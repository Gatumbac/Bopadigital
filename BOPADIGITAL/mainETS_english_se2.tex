\chapter{INTRODUCTION AND PROJECT CONTEXT}

\section{Project Context}
BOHORQUEZ \& PAUTA CORP. BOPACORP S.A. is a business-to-business (B2B) company that provides telecommunications products and services to corporate clients. Its commercial operation depends on sales advisors who prospect for clients, schedule visits, manage negotiations, collect supporting documentation, and follow up on service activation. Supervisors and managers also need updated information about the sales pipeline, field activity, documentation status, and commercial performance.

Before BOPADIGITAL, many activities relied on spreadsheets, shared folders, messaging applications, and email. These disconnected tools made it difficult to maintain a timely and unified view of each client and negotiation. The project was therefore defined as a digital platform to centralize the commercial lifecycle and support all users involved in the process.

The project context and initial requirements were established during Software Engineering I. The requirements document identified the business problem, stakeholders, functional and non-functional requirements, use cases, prototypes, and initial risks. The client accepted that requirements baseline on November 4, 2025. During Software Engineering II, the team continued the implementation through successive Scrum iterations and delivered the final documentation and deployment evidence.

\section{Problem Statement}
The initial analysis identified three operational bottlenecks in BOPACORP's commercial process:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Document delivery and operational delay:} After closing or advancing a sale, advisors may need to collect contracts, identification documents, taxpayer information, and other files from the client. When these documents are handled physically or through separate channels, the advisor may need to return to the office, creating avoidable dead time and delaying the operational review.
    \item \textbf{Limited visibility of field activity:} Supervisors and management do not have a centralized view of the visits, negotiation progress, and activities performed by each advisor. Monitoring therefore depends on individual messages and manual status updates.
    \item \textbf{Manual sales tracking and reporting:} Client portfolios, negotiations, and performance indicators were previously consolidated through spreadsheets and manual reports. This increases the effort required for follow-up meetings and makes it more difficult to use current information for decision-making.
\end{enumerate}

BOPADIGITAL addresses these bottlenecks by connecting public information, internal commercial management, document workflows, and reporting capabilities through a shared software platform.

\section{Project Description and Evolution}
BOPADIGITAL is an integrated digital platform composed of a public website, an internal CRM, a mobile advisor application, a centralized REST API, and shared TypeScript contracts and validation schemas. The public website supports the presentation of services, contact with the commercial team, content administration, and employability workflows. The internal applications support client and negotiation management, visits, documents, supervision, reporting, and role-based access.

The mobile repository, \texttt{bopacorp-mobile}, uses Expo and React Native and contains advisor-oriented routes for authentication, clients, negotiations, documentation, and settings. The verified mobile workflows are documented together with the corresponding runtime evidence in the testing and demonstration chapters.

The project evolved from the requirements and prototypes produced in Software Engineering I into an implementation and delivery process in Software Engineering II. The later stages added automated quality checks, frontend and API testing, risk-based testing documentation, deployment and installation guides, user manuals, and final acceptance evidence.

\section{Project Objectives}
The general objective is \textbf{to digitize and centralize} BOPACORP's B2B commercial lifecycle in order to improve operational efficiency, information availability, and managerial visibility.

The specific objectives are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{To support} the registration and management of corporate clients and commercial prospects.
    \item \textbf{To track} negotiations, visits, statuses, and relevant commercial observations.
    \item \textbf{To enable} advisors to manage field information and supporting documentation through the available web and mobile workflows.
    \item \textbf{To centralize} the review and approval status of documents required by the commercial process.
    \item \textbf{To provide} supervisors and managers with consolidated information for monitoring and decision-making.
    \item \textbf{To offer} a public service catalog, a content management interface, and an employability workflow for external users and candidates.
    \item \textbf{To enforce} authentication, authorization, shared data contracts, automated quality checks, and reproducible deployment practices.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Project Scope and Exclusions}
The final scope is organized around the following components:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Public Website:} public company information, service catalog, service details, contact with the commercial team, and employability workflows.
    \item \textbf{Content Management System:} authorized management of public content, catalog information, and related media.
    \item \textbf{CRM:} role-based management of clients, negotiations, visits, documents, supervision, reports, organization, and employability information.
    \item \textbf{Mobile Advisor Application:} mobile access to advisor-oriented client, negotiation, and documentation workflows implemented in the Expo/React Native repository.
    \item \textbf{REST API and Shared Contracts:} authentication, authorization, business services, persistence integration, request validation, and shared TypeScript/Zod contracts.
    \item \textbf{Deployment and Documentation:} continuous integration, deployment configuration, installation instructions, user manuals, testing evidence, and client acceptance records.
\end{itemize}

The Offer Matrices module was removed from the final functional scope by decision of the client. Consequently, matrix construction, matrix approval, subsidy calculations, and related calculations are not presented as final BOPADIGITAL features or demonstration scenarios. Historical requirements, diagrams, or code references to this module are treated as legacy context or residual technical material.

The final report presents the capabilities included in the agreed scope and supported by the project evidence package. Capabilities outside that scope are treated as limitations or future work and are not used to support the acceptance scenarios.

\section{Stakeholders, Roles and Target Users}
The main stakeholders and target users are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Business Client:} explores the public service catalog and contacts BOPACORP to start a commercial conversation.
    \item \textbf{Sales Advisor:} manages assigned clients, negotiations, visits, observations, and supporting documents during the commercial process.
    \item \textbf{Supervisor or Sales Manager:} monitors the work of advisors, reviews commercial progress, and supervises pending activities and documents.
    \item \textbf{Documentation Coordinator:} reviews the documentation associated with negotiations and updates its status for operational processing.
    \item \textbf{Management or Executive Board:} consults consolidated reports and performance information for strategic decisions.
    \item \textbf{Sales Advisor Candidate:} reviews job vacancies and submits an application through the public website.
    \item \textbf{Web Administrator:} maintains public website content, catalog data, and related CMS information.
    \item \textbf{Client Representative:} reviews sprint increments, provides feedback, and signs the corresponding acceptance records.
\end{itemize}

\section{Repository Ecosystem and System Boundaries}
The final BOPADIGITAL solution is organized as a coordinated ecosystem of repositories instead of a single application. This organization separates public communication, internal commercial management, field work, backend services, shared contracts, and deployment operations. The separation also allows each part of the system to be developed and validated according to its own technical responsibilities while preserving a common integration boundary through the REST API.

The system boundary is therefore defined by the clients, the API, the shared contract package, and the persistence and external-service layer. The clients do not contain database credentials and do not issue SQL queries. They communicate with the API through the versioned path \emph{/api/v1}. The API is responsible for enforcing the rules that must remain consistent across web, CRM, and mobile clients.

The architecture follows a client--server boundary with a modular-monolith backend. This choice avoids duplicating business rules across several frontends while preserving independent user interfaces for public users, administrators, sales advisors, supervisors, coordinators, and managers. The resulting structure is simpler to deploy than a group of independent backend services, but it still provides internal separation through domain modules and one-way dependencies.

\section{Technology Selection}
The technology decisions were evaluated according to the nature of the project, the existing team skills, the need for rapid iteration during Scrum sprints, the number of client applications, and the requirement to demonstrate a complete and maintainable product. The following comparisons document the engineering rationale for the final stack. They are retrospective justifications of the implemented architecture and do not claim that every alternative was formally prototyped.

The API accesses the database through Drizzle and environment-provided connection settings, while the database location is separated from the shared development service. Supabase is therefore not presented as a production dependency. The deployment appendix documents the corresponding provisioning, migrations, access control, backup, and recovery procedures.

\subsection{Token-Based Authentication}
During login, the API normalizes the email, applies rate limiting, retrieves the active user, compares the password using bcrypt, records the login result, and issues an access token together with an opaque refresh token. The access token is a short-lived JWT, while the refresh token is stored as a SHA-256 hash in the database. This limits the exposure period of the access token and allows refresh sessions to be revoked during logout, password changes, user deactivation, or token-rotation replay detection.

\subsection{Permission Enforcement}
The \emph{authenticate} middleware extracts the bearer token, verifies the JWT signature, loads the active user and roles, and attaches the authenticated identity to the request. The \emph{authorize} middleware then checks the permission codes required by the route.

Permissions use the \emph{module.action} convention. Examples include \emph{users.read}, \emph{catalog\_items.create}, and \emph{content\_blocks.update}. A valid identity without the required permission receives a forbidden response, while a missing or invalid identity receives an unauthorized response.

\subsection{Layered Protection}
The authorization design is role-based, but the route checks are expressed as permissions. This allows a role to group several capabilities while keeping the endpoint declaration explicit. Validation occurs after the authorization decision, preventing unauthorized requests from receiving unnecessary detailed validation feedback. Services remain responsible for ownership and state-dependent rules, such as whether a user may view or modify a record associated with a particular organization or workflow.

\section{Shared Contracts and Data Architecture}
The shared contract package is the integration boundary between the API and its clients. It provides common primitives, request schemas, response schemas, enumerations, and API response wrappers. The package is published as \emph{@bopacorp/shared} and is consumed by the API, public website, CRM, and mobile repository.

The request path is divided into four responsibilities. First, the route selects the endpoint and attaches middleware. Second, the controller reads already-validated request data and delegates the operation. Third, the service applies business rules and performs Drizzle queries. Finally, the database persists or retrieves the data. This one-way dependency flow is represented as:

\begin{center}
\emph{routes} $\rightarrow$ \emph{controller} $\rightarrow$ \emph{service} $\rightarrow$ \emph{database}
\end{center}

The API uses a common response envelope. A successful operation returns \emph{success: true} and its data; a paginated operation also returns metadata; and an error returns \emph{success: false} with a stable error code and message. The convention makes error handling predictable for all clients and prevents each frontend from implementing a different interpretation of HTTP responses.

The persistence architecture uses PostgreSQL schemas and tables accessed through Drizzle ORM. The API modules represent business concerns rather than reproducing the database schema one-to-one. For example, authentication, users, and roles use related identity and permission tables, while catalog operations may span catalog and CMS data. This distinction keeps the public API organized around user-facing responsibilities rather than exposing internal storage details.

The final scope deliberately excludes the matrix-construction and matrix-approval module. Although historical requirements and some shared-package exports may mention matrices, they are not part of the final demonstrated architecture or acceptance scenarios. The report therefore treats them as excluded or future work instead of using them to justify current system behavior.

Inside the API boundary, Express receives the request and applies cross-cutting middleware before routing it to a domain module.
